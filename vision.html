Chapter 05 · Computer vision

An AI that spots litter in a street photo, built from the dataset to the phone app.

C du Propre lets a resident photograph litter in the street. A model finds each item and names it, the resident confirms, and the report goes to the town with its location. Five of us built it in spring 2023, during the KESK'IA programme. I worked on every stage: the data, the training, the API in Docker and the Android app.

Team of fiveTrappes, FranceSpring 2023National winner, KESK'IA

It finds.

Four items in one photo, each in its own box, down to a bottle cap a few centimetres wide.

It names.

Two clear plastic bottles and a bottle cap, correctly. It calls the paper bag Other carton: close, not right.

It asks.

That is why the screen ends on Confirm. The resident checks the answer before anything is sent.

End to end

Seven steps, from an open dataset to a phone.

KESK'IA gave each team a brief, a public dataset, starter notebooks and mentors. Its plan ended on a web dashboard. We also built the phone app, because the person who sees the litter is standing in the street.

  1. 01

    Start from open data

    We began with TACO, an open dataset of litter photographed in streets, woods and on beaches: 1,500 photos and about 4,800 labelled items, in 60 categories.

    TACO
  2. 02

    Read the data first

    Before any training, I counted the labels in each category. Cigarette butts and "unlabelled litter" dominated. Several categories had one or two examples, and one had none. A model cannot learn something it has seen twice.

    Python · notebooks
  3. 03

    Prepare it

    I converted the labels to the format the model expects, one text file per photo. Every image was resized to 640 pixels, then rotated, blurred and re-exposed to triple the training set, to about 3,600 images.

    pycocotools · Roboflow
  4. 04

    Fine-tune a model

    We fine-tuned YOLOv5, a pretrained detection model with about 21 million parameters, on a cloud GPU. One run of 60 passes over the data took under two hours.

    PyTorch · YOLOv5 · AWS SageMaker
  5. 05

    Go outside

    The dataset's photos come from all over the world. Our mentors pushed us to add our own, so we photographed litter in the streets of Trappes, with French packaging on French pavements, and labelled it.

    Field photos
  6. 06

    Serve it

    The trained model sits behind a small web API: a photo goes in, the boxes and names come back. It is packaged in a Docker image and deployed as a serverless service.

    Flask · Docker · Cloud Run
  7. 07

    Put it in a hand

    GreenAI, the Android app at the top of this page: take a photo, see what the model found, confirm, send.

    Android

What we learned

Fewer categories, a better model.

With 60 categories and so few examples of most of them, the first model mostly guessed. So we merged the look-alikes and dropped what the data could not support. The score improved as the list got shorter.

  • 60The dataset as published.
  • 28Grouped by family, the rarest ones removed.
  • 13Bottle, can, carton, cup, cigarette and eight more.

How well it finds each category

  • Bottle cap0.66
  • Can0.54
  • Carton0.37
  • Bottle0.36
  • Cup0.30
  • Other litter0.21
  • Cigarette0.13
  • All 130.26

Average precision at 0.5 overlap, from 0 to 1, on 150 photos the model had never seen. A 13-category run from late April 2023, half-way through the project. Seven of the thirteen categories are shown.

  • Rigid shapes work. Caps and cans look the same everywhere.
  • Cigarette butts do not, yet. Most likely because they shrink to a few pixels once the photo is resized.
  • What we planned from it: more street photos, some photos with no litter at all to cut false alarms, and longer training.

June 2023

Then we took it on stage.

C du Propre was one of three projects picked out of 22 at VivaTech, then won the KESK'IA national final at Société Générale a week later. In December it took me to UC Berkeley.

The team stayed together: we went on to found OSMOZ, to open AI to young people in Trappes.
